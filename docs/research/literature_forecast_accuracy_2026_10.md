## Recommendation and scope

Consider a **small nonlinear replacement for the football model's conditional residual head** first. Consider a **hierarchical conditional residual head** as an alternative, not a second simultaneous change. Both would test a different estimator on existing causal information while preserving the separately modeled participation and scoring components. The current residual is Ridge fitted on appearances (`src/squadopt/prediction/football.py:94-97`); the existing features already include recent minutes, starts, player attacking rates and own/opponent strength (`src/squadopt/prediction/football_features.py:15-68`). These are research suggestions, not accepted candidates.

The strongest immediately relevant outside FPL study is OpenFPL [E1], but its seven prospective weeks, feature sources and validation design do not establish a gain here. News, workload, projected units and tactical models remain relevant literature topics, but their existing laboratory preparations are not new proposals. A game-forecast blend already has a dedicated research lane (`docs/research/README.md:17-20`).

Review date: 15 October 2026. Search coverage includes primary papers, author manuscripts and official scoring explanations on FPL point forecasting, minutes/injury, player and team event models, ensemble forecasts and distribution scoring. Queries combined these topics with FPL, Bayesian, XGBoost, availability and calibration. This is a targeted review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Commercial accuracy claims without an inspectable population and comparator were excluded. No paper's leaderboard rank is treated as a paired forecast experiment.

Repository references below are pinned to `develop` commit `792bef77f9583c8a4a88d8445d246ed341f30814`. They describe that tree, not a newly inspected private capture or a deployment verification. All empirical repository numbers are copied from committed narrative records. No raw archive, private capture, OOF expansion or new settled result was read.

## Leakage and evaluation conditions common to every method

**Archive inputs.** Historical minutes, goals, assists, saves, bonus, cards and source xG/xA are outcomes, usable only after lagging; the dictionary lists them as required or optional, not as uniformly complete (`docs/data_dictionary.md:118-169`). Stable player identity is `code`; historical prices are shifted because timing is undocumented. Archived `xP` is explicitly inadmissible as a feature (`docs/data_pipeline.md:379-398`). Historical fixture capture times and deadline times are absent, and postponement history cannot be recovered (`docs/data_pipeline.md:443-462`). These deficiencies cannot be fixed by renaming columns.

**Live inputs.** A preserved immutable capture can establish the time of its prices, news and status; captures are never rewritten (`docs/data_pipeline.md:490-551`). That does not establish complete longitudinal injury, projected lineup, event, betting or all-competition data. Weekly xG cannot be split into fixture-level double-gameweek observations when that split is ambiguous (`docs/live-football-model.md:47-57`).

**Folds.** Any later historical comparison must fit only `rows_before`, build shifted features using the deadline-known part of `rows_through`, and score only after freezing the decision. No random split, future normalization or backfill is admissible (`docs/data_pipeline.md:53-110`, `docs/data_pipeline.md:196-250`). Inner tuning must also advance through time. Use a named historical scoring regime and admitted seasons; do not silently pool the older non-DEFCON evidence with live totals or treat the football development authorization as general v1 holdout permission (`docs/measurements_index.md:56-91`, `docs/prediction_research_agenda.md:3-20`). This review chooses no new training population.

**Gate G, for every predictive candidate below.** At least two judged seasons; within-position pooled macro Spearman gain at least +0.01 with no seasonal or pooled position regression beyond 0.01; all-row MAE at most 5 percent above control in every season and pooled; realized paired squad gain at least +0.5 per decision, with a strictly positive lower end of the declared paired 90 percent interval and at most one losing season. Same roster, scoring and deterministic solver budget; report incomplete pairs and statuses (`docs/prediction_ranking_gate.md:8-41`). Appeared-row gains, an injury classifier's precision, a shot Brier score or a distribution score cannot substitute for G.

The existing football prospective study instead binds its exact candidate, served comparator, capture choice, missing weeks and GW20/GW38 readings. It cannot be repurposed to judge these suggestions, and its one-season rule does not supply G's second season (`docs/football_prospective_prereg.md:59-110`, `docs/football_prospective_prereg.md:184-205`, `docs/football_prospective_prereg.md:228-276`). A future owner-selected study would need its own accepted design. No sketch here freezes one.

## Literature and method assessment

Each method's last sentence gives its evaluation route and its relation to already recorded work. Feasibility means source fields are documented, not that their coverage or admission has been newly checked.

### 1. Position-specific tree ensembles and conditional residual correction

**Published evidence.** Groos's OpenFPL [E1] combines position-specific Random Forest/XGBoost models using FPL and Understat histories. Development covers 2020-21 to 2023-24; prospective evaluation is only 2024-25 GW32-38. Table 4 reports one-week RMSE 1.517 versus Last 5's 2.136 on 3-4 point returns, and 5.142 versus 5.613 on 5+ returns. On non-players, OpenFPL is worse: 0.818 versus 0.791, and commercial FPL Review is 0.689. Development folds split by club, and missing 2020-21 Understat values borrow from later seasons. Those choices are unsuitable for this repository's temporal test. [E1, sections 2.2-3 and Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09992v1).

**Application judgment.** Adapt the estimator idea, not that dataset recipe: replace only the existing conditional residual head with shallow boosting on its existing causal features. Retain minutes, club intensities, allocations, CS and DEFCON, so changes remain interpretable. The residual is already a distinct contribution in the point composition (`src/squadopt/prediction/football.py:94-97`, `src/squadopt/prediction/football.py:215-228`). Do not copy outcome-based return categories into inference or balanced target weights without checking their effect on a mean forecast.

**Inputs, scoring and novelty.** The documented feature set and residual target support a restricted version now; PPDA, detailed shots and Understat coverage from the paper are not established by that contract (`docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:136-144`, `src/squadopt/prediction/football_features.py:15-68`). Use G, plus conditional residual MAE/bias on regulars and a full-roster regression check. This is an estimator replacement, not `prior_minutes_level`'s bucket multiplier, the rejected learned-rate production candidate or #1053's additional workload/venue features (`docs/measurements_index.md:111`, `docs/measurements_index.md:215`; [#1053 disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1053#issuecomment-6167289548)). Rank: **1 among new choices**. It does not address the non-appearance head.

**Calendar and schedule variants.** A tree can take known future fixture counts and venue alongside past form; that is an adaptation, not an isolated published effect from E1. Counts distinguish blanks and doubles, while raw archived difficulty is not admitted just because it is numeric (`docs/data_pipeline.md:473-488`). Calendar-day workload/venue expansion already has #1053's preparation. Hold those extra features out of the first estimator question; a later schedule-feature ablation would still need G and causal calendar snapshots, not observed future postponements ([#1053 disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1053#issuecomment-6167289548); `docs/data_pipeline.md:454-462`).

### 2. Hierarchical player models, form and regression

**Published evidence.** Egidi and Gabry [E2] use hierarchical Bayesian models for Italian fantasy ratings, explicitly modeling missing appearances; the first half of Serie A 2015-16 trains second-half predictions. Their author-hosted proceedings explain player, team and position nesting, not an FPL decision gain. No transferable points-per-week effect is available from that evidence. [E2, author deposit](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3253949?mode=complete).

**Application judgment.** A hierarchical conditional residual head could estimate player deviations with uncertainty-dependent pooling toward position, rather than raising all regulars by a bucket factor. This is an alternative to method 1. Modeling an already identified player's residual persistence is the question; a famous finisher is not automatically granted a permanent multiplier. Existing football rate features already shrink player totals toward positional priors with ten prior exposures, so generic partial pooling alone is not novel (`src/squadopt/prediction/football_features.py:130-163`).

**Inputs, scoring and novelty.** Stable identities and prior point components exist in the documented historical design; missing appearances and genuine zeros must remain distinct (`docs/data_pipeline.md:379-398`, `docs/data_dictionary.md:118-145`). Use G and the same regular/cold-start diagnostics as method 1. Estimate pooling only within earlier training data. The ledger records recent-luck residual structure, but not proof that a player random effect adds useful residual persistence (`docs/measurements_index.md:204`). This differs from the rejected decision-side bucket correction and from new-player price/ownership refits that worsened ordering (`docs/measurements_index.md:173-174`, `docs/measurements_index.md:215`). Rank: **2**, with less direct FPL evidence than method 1.

**Time-series alternative.** Gupta [E12] ensembles ARIMA and recurrent-network point forecasts using three prior seasons and optimizes a squad with linear programming. The full text, open on arXiv, tracks the selected fifteen over 2016-17 and reports one team-level figure: their 3718-point forecast total was 87 points too high. Its only other error figures are 2015-16 validation RMSEs for two players, used to set a 0.4/0.6 ARIMA/LSTM blend; there is no baseline, paired error or decision effect. That supports surveying form dynamics, not expecting a gain. Lagged point/minute histories support a restricted analogue here (`docs/data_pipeline.md:53-110`), but the current model already reads recent form (`src/squadopt/prediction/football_features.py:45-56`). Defer a full recurrent model behind the two smaller alternatives; use G, an expanding chronological fit and unchanged seasonal eligibility if the owner later selects it. Budget zero credited gain on either audit gap before that comparison. [E12](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12938).

### 3. Availability, rotation, injury news and workload

**Published evidence.** Rossi et al. [E3] predict next-session/game injuries from an elite club's GPS training workload. In 10,000 repeated two-fold cross-validations their decision tree averages 50 percent precision and 80 percent recall, against below 4 percent precision for the ACWR and monotony forecasters and at most about 6 percent for four simple baselines. In a week-by-week simulation of one season it reaches precision 0.56 and F1 0.60, detecting 9 of 14 injuries from week 6. This is injury prediction with private physical measurements, not a public-data weekly start or minutes forecast. [E3, introduction and evaluation](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0201264).

**Inputs and judgment.** Public FPL histories are not GPS training exposure. Recorded live news can support a different availability study, but it cannot recreate predeadline historical injuries. Existing start-label problems include zero-filled early 2022-23 and only one judged season in the earlier participation composition (`docs/measurements_index.md:216-217`). PL-only load is not all-competition load; complete participation, travel and original forecast receipts remain explicit requirements (`docs/research/football_load_minutes_development.md:27-68`).

Use G plus appearance Brier/log loss, conditional-start and minute-bin scores, missing-source counts and the selected-player zero-minute rate. **Do not propose the existing flag or workload models again**: #1063/#1065 merged synthetic preparation and closed their native-combination programmes, not a negative forecasting measurement ([flag disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1063#issuecomment-6167292082), [workload disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1065#issuecomment-6167292883)). Plain isotonic appearance recalibration already failed G's ordering/decision requirements (`docs/measurements_index.md:136`). Rank: **existing/deferred**, not a new selected candidate.

### 4. Player xG/xA, fixture scoring and projected lineups

**Published evidence.** Bayes-xG [E4] studies position/player effects using StatsBomb shot events. Its non-Bayesian feature comparison reports Brier 0.086 for the basic model and 0.076 for the richer model over 63,309 shots, versus StatsBomb 0.075. That comparison is not a prospective FPL gain or a quantified benefit from hierarchical player effects. It needs shot geometry and freeze-frame context. [E4, sections 3-4 and Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.13707).

Whitaker et al. [E5] infer player abilities for event types from EPL touch-by-touch data, add them to the Baio and Blangiardo team goal model, and predict whether 2014-15 EPL matches go over 2.5 goals. They refit in expanding blocks on all of 2013-14 plus the earlier 2014-15 games, and use expert-predicted starting line-ups (86 percent accurate over the season). The player model raised over/under AUC in all five prediction blocks (for example 0.68 against 0.55 in block 4) and the betting return. Its predictive goal log-likelihood on the second 80-game block of 2014-15 was worse than the team baseline's (-163.1 against -159.6), and the authors report similar results for the other blocks. None of this is an FPL squad gain. The event and projected-lineup inputs are material parts of the method, not implementation details. [E5, sections 4.1-4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.00001).

**Inputs, scoring and duplication.** Source xG/xA aggregates are documented, but shot locations, pressure, actual units and historically timed projected legal units are not implied by them (`docs/data_dictionary.md:167-169`, `docs/research/football_projected_unit_strength.md:10-55`). The football model already allocates team goals and assists through player rates (`docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:19-27`). Use G with count deviance, allocation conservation and FPL-credit diagnostics; source-provider assists must not be assumed to equal credited FPL assists (`docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:33-42`). Defer shot-source expansion. Projected units, duty changes and tactical interactions overlap closed preparations, rather than a fresh unused idea ([#1057](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1057), [#1059 disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1059#issuecomment-6167290358), [#1061 disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1061#issuecomment-6167291100)). Rank: **source-gated/deferred**.

### 5. Clean sheets, fixture difficulty and changing team strength

**Published evidence.** Egidi, Pauli and Torelli [E6] combine historical team parameters and betting information in a hierarchical Poisson model, fitting nine seasons and predicting a tenth across major European leagues. The paper reports comparative predictive checks, not a transferable FPL decision uplift. Its relevance is learning team scoring rates from complementary information, not applying an extra difficulty multiplier. [E6, model and evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08848).

**Inputs and judgment.** Captured calendar structure is useful, but archived difficulty and end-season strength are not automatically deadline-safe (`docs/data_pipeline.md:443-488`, `docs/measurements_index.md:196-197`). Current own/opponent outcome histories support causal team models; a market channel requires a new admitted timestamped source. G must be accompanied by goal log score, CS Brier and high-CS calibration, with exposure over actual minute support. A good team-goal score alone cannot pass the decision gate.

Do not resurrect the simple difficulty overlay, Dixon-Coles post-adjustment, full-match CS approximation or positional-defence formula: the recorded negative decision/consistency results remain binding (`docs/measurements_index.md:175-177`, `docs/measurements_index.md:197`, `docs/measurements_index.md:294`, `docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:7-10`). Score-state dynamics are also an existing closed preparation ([#1067 disposition](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1067#issuecomment-6167293739)). Rank: **deferred**, pending information genuinely beyond those failed specifications.

### 6. Defensive contributions and the current scoring rules

**Official evidence.** The Premier League's 2026-27 explanation awards two points for 10 defender CBIT actions or 12 midfielder/forward CBIRT actions, with recoveries included for the latter group [E7]. This is a scoring definition, with no reported forecasting-method effect. [E7, official glossary](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2683145).

**Inputs, scoring and duplication.** Historical v1 canonical data does not provide a general DEFCON training label. The football model already uses a minute-mixed defensive-count tail; direct-tail shrinkage has a recorded development comparison (`docs/measurements_index.md:80-91`, `docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:73-77`, `docs/research/football_defcon_development.md:10-39`). Missing historical defensive labels are not zeros, and one live season cannot satisfy a two-season total-point gate. Use event Brier/log loss and calibration around the threshold as diagnostics, but retain G for an eventual full point candidate. Rank: **existing lane**, not a new DEFCON proposal.

### 7. Blending with game, crowd and market forecasts

**Published evidence.** Bonello et al. [E8] combine historical statistics, betting and human/social information, reporting over 300 points and about 11 points per week above their statistical comparator in 2018-19. That is a multi-input system result, not an isolated blend effect. Their section 3.3 permits a fresh lineup each week without real transfer penalties, so the quoted rank is not a legal season-chain result. Matthews et al. [E9] report approximately top-percentile performance against 2.5 million managers for a Bayesian sequential manager trained on 2009-10 and evaluated on 2010-11. Neither identifies a +11 point forecast improvement under today's rules. [E8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.07441), [E9](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8259/8118).

**Inputs and judgment.** A same-capture game forecast is a plausible complementary signal; archived `xP` is not a substitute. Historical ownership timing is unverified, while a captured elite cohort has a distinct evidence contract (`docs/data_pipeline.md:396-398`, `docs/measurements_index.md:112`, `docs/measurements_index.md:245`). A new betting blend needs a rights-admitted, predeadline quote store with market definition, margin treatment and missingness; the documented native football inputs establish none of that (`docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:136-144`).

Use G, preserve the control where a source is missing, and assess changes separately on regulars, unflagged non-appearers and selectable players. A public blend's current one-season study is already owned by #1004, not a new candidate in this record (`docs/research/README.md:17-20`; [#1004](https://github.com/MyManDev/football-squad-optimizer/issues/1004)). The Top100 utility setting is already present and is not a measured probability correction (`docs/live-football-model.md:11-13`). Rank: **highest relevance among existing lanes**, no duplicate protocol.

### 8. Full point distributions rather than means alone

**Published evidence.** NGBoost [E10] learns conditional distribution parameters with natural-gradient boosting and evaluates probabilistic regression on non-football benchmarks. Gneiting and Raftery [E11] explain proper scoring rules, including logarithmic score and CRPS, and why coverage and narrowness alone do not determine forecast quality. Neither reports an FPL gain. [E10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03225), [E11](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf).

**Application judgment.** A fresh distributional residual model could represent skew, atoms and negative points conditional on appearance, then combine with the existing football events. A Gaussian total-points interval is not an adequate representation of absence, cameo and haul states. This is a longer research direction after choosing a mean-model question, not a reason to enlarge the current assignment.

**Inputs, scoring and novelty.** Historical negative scores are valid; the football simulator currently keeps residual bonus/card effects as conditional means and assumes independent player minute draws (`docs/data_dictionary.md:132-145`, `docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:33-42`). Use CRPS/log score, zero/negative/tail event calibration, and selected-squad PIT/tail diagnostics on chronological data. If a method claims better recommendations it must also pass G; a spread-only change cannot claim a mean-point gain from improved coverage alone. This differs from retuning the failed squad sampler or selecting its already screened dispersion scale (`docs/measurements_index.md:125`, `docs/measurements_index.md:207-209`). Rank: **3 among new directions**, with lower near-term decision value than the two single-head alternatives.

## Estimated value on the audit's gaps

These are deliberately modest **planning judgments**, not fitted effects, confidence intervals, power calculations or gate results. The numerical anchors are the printed per-week audit and existing oracle, not newly joined rows. A negative decision effect remains possible for every method. No positive lower bound or +0.5 decision gain can honestly be forecast from this literature.

| Choice | Honest scale estimate and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| 1, nonlinear conditional residual | Worth considering if it recovers about 0.05-0.15 points per prior-60+ player per week, a small portion of the audit's +0.405/+0.556 regular bias. This is an elicited target magnitude, not an expectation justified by OpenFPL. Predictable recovery may be zero. It leaves the appearance mechanism fixed, so expect no direct absent-mass repair (`docs/live_projection_audit.md:116`, `docs/live_projection_audit.md:165`; `src/squadopt/prediction/football.py:94-97`). |
| 2, hierarchical conditional residual | A smaller 0-0.10 points per regular is a reasonable budget assumption until residual persistence is established; it can also shrink a real scorer's edge away. Same audit anchor and no direct absence benefit. The already present ten-exposure rate shrinkage limits the novelty (`docs/live_projection_audit.md:116`, `docs/live_projection_audit.md:165`; `src/squadopt/prediction/football_features.py:130-163`). |
| Existing game blend | Illustrative arithmetic only: a one-quarter blend on the printed timely GW4 regular totals adds `(685.8-558.1)/4 = 31.925` forecast points against a 74.9-point deficit; GW5 adds `(638.4-522.4)/4 = 29.0` against 99.6. On all non-appearers it removes about 6.4 and 7.3 forecast points from the printed totals. Those are retrospective movements, not error or decision gains; the weight is not proposed or selected (`docs/live_projection_audit.md:95`, `docs/live_projection_audit.md:116-124`, `docs/live_projection_audit.md:144`, `docs/live_projection_audit.md:165-173`). |
| Rotation/news/load literature | Zero defensible numerical injury-to-FPL conversion without the missing sources. The recorded perfect oracle's +1.1088 per decision is context, not an attainable ceiling for every possible minutes model, and it is not additive to the above gains (`docs/measurements_index.md:219`). |
| Shot xG, new market inputs, tactical/units/score-state, CS | Budget zero credited decision gain until source admission and an isolated prospective comparison exist. Do not attach outside shot-Brier or team-goal improvements to the audit's regular deficit. Existing causal-rate, component and negative-ablation results already show why that inference fails (`docs/measurements_index.md:102-103`, `docs/measurements_index.md:197`, `docs/measurements_index.md:294`). |
| DEFCON | At most two points per eligible player-fixture from this component under E7's rule. This is a rule maximum, not recoverable forecast bias; historical regime mismatch prevents estimating an incremental live gain from old totals (`docs/measurements_index.md:80-91`). |
| 3, distributional residual | Budget zero mean-point improvement from dispersion alone. Its immediate research value is correctly scoring the whole distribution; the existing lower-tail failures prohibit simply calling the current scenarios calibrated (`docs/measurements_index.md:207-209`). |

The estimates are not additive: the two new mean heads are alternatives, a blend can move the same players, and minutes affect both conditional exposure and attacking allocation. The existing model's composition already links these terms (`docs/research/football_candidate_development.md:73-77`).
